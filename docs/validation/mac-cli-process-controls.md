## Safety and evidence

Launch at most three synthetic peers concurrently, each with a 256 MiB maximum
Java heap. Observe owned-lifetime RSS, CPU counters, threads and descriptors at
phase boundaries and approximately five-second intervals; stop on a 1 GiB RSS,
512-thread, 2,048-descriptor, 8 MiB terminal-log or 2,700-second campaign cutoff.
Resource-pressure peers additionally lower **only their own** soft descriptor
and process limits to at most 256 and 512. The process hard limit is also bounded
by the read-only `kern.maxprocperuid` ceiling that Darwin applies on `setrlimit`;
both values must read back exactly. No parent or system limit is raised or changed.
This is not descriptor/process-quota
exhaustion. Keep at least 6 GiB disk headroom at admission; never fill the disk or
purge owner data. Fixture and transfer byte budgets are explicit in the source.

Fault delivery requires a live direct child, matching UID/real UID and kernel
lifetime/parent identity, and its captured opaque audit token. An exec change or
denied signal fails closed: no raw PID kill, name scan, token fabrication or
fallback authority. The enclosing original native executor owns final cleanup.

Retain per-case results, native receipts, child limits, source/runtime manifest,
stdin/fault timeline, terminal logs, resources, immutable pre-fault ZIP snapshots,
final diagnostic exports and receiver filesystem/hash observations. Verify ZIP
member bounds/checksums and source/session membership without extraction. The
existing exporter samples summary and events separately; do not claim an atomic
event-count snapshot. Intentional KILL is never labeled graceful cleanup. Empty
destination reservations and staging left by a crash are not successful files;
preserve them for reconciliation. Catchable exits must retire staging/reservations.
